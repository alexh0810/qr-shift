# QRShift Landing Page Outline

## Hero

### Goal

Create an above-the-fold product moment that feels bold and memorable while still explaining QRShift within a few seconds.

### Required message shape

- Headline must communicate the core value immediately:
  - QR-based clock in and clock out
  - no app installs or staff logins
  - payroll-ready reporting
- Subheadline must translate the value into a practical business outcome for small businesses with hourly staff.
- The hero should feel product-forward, not like a generic brochure or internal dashboard header.

### Required elements

- `BrandMark`
- primary CTA: `Request a Demo`
- secondary public CTA: `Contact`
- static product preview placed early in the visual hierarchy
- small brand-supporting label or proof chip, used sparingly

## How It Works

### Goal

Explain the QRShift workflow with minimal cognitive load so visitors understand the operational loop quickly.

### Structure

Use a 3-step or 4-step sequence. Each step should have:

- one short action phrase
- one supporting sentence
- one clear business outcome

### Recommended workflow

1. Create a QR clock station
2. Staff scan to clock in or out
3. Managers see who is on shift
4. Reports are exportable for payroll

## Core Benefits

### Goal

Show why QRShift is useful for small businesses with hourly staff without drifting into vague SaaS copy.

### Required benefit themes

- Faster shift visibility
- No apps or login friction for staff
- Cleaner payroll-ready reporting

### Content rules

- Benefits must stay concrete and product-specific
- Avoid enterprise, platform, or transformation language
- Each benefit block should tie back to attendance or payroll clarity

## Best-Fit Businesses

### Goal

Help visitors quickly self-identify whether QRShift is for them.

### Required audience framing

Keep the section focused on small, single-location businesses with hourly staff, especially:

- bubble tea shops and small cafes
- casual restaurants and takeaway counters
- nail, beauty, and salon businesses
- small retail shops and kiosks

### Messaging rule

This section should confirm fit, not expand scope into unsupported verticals or enterprise cases.

## CTA Close

### Goal

End the page with a confident conversion prompt that restates the value and repeats the public actions.

### Required CTA structure

- primary CTA: `Request a Demo`
- secondary public CTA: `Contact`
- short supporting reminder of the value proposition

### Placement rule

The closing CTA should feel like a natural finish to the page narrative, not a duplicate of the hero with no added context.

## Product Preview

### Goal

Make QRShift feel like a real, working product through a static interface preview.

### Direction

The preview should feel like a believable QRShift dashboard and reporting surface rather than a generic SaaS mockup.

### Required visual cues

- live attendance or shift-status signals
- payroll/reporting cues
- structured surfaces using the Phase 1 brand language
- QRShift branding or contextual labels that make the interface feel product-specific

### Avoid

- generic browser chrome without product cues
- decorative placeholder charts with no relevance to attendance
- interactive fake controls that imply behavior not implemented

## CTA Hierarchy

### Public actions

- `Request a Demo` is the primary CTA
- `Contact` is the secondary public CTA

### Internal or founder action

- `/admin/create-store` remains available
- it must be treated as tertiary and internal/demo-oriented
- it must not visually compete with public conversion actions

### Hierarchy rule

Visitors should read the page as customer-facing first. Founder/demo access is preserved for workflow convenience, not treated as the main purpose of the route.

## Copy Guardrails

- The page must explain QRShift within 10 seconds of landing.
- `How It Works` should use a 3-step or 4-step workflow narrative.
- Language must stay practical and concrete for small businesses with hourly staff.
- Use product-specific phrasing tied to QR attendance, live visibility, and payroll-ready reporting.
- Keep `Request a Demo` and `Contact` visible as explicit public actions.

### Banned phrases

- `streamline your workforce`
- `transform operations`
- `next-generation workforce platform`
- `all-in-one people solution`

### Preferred tone

- direct
- clean
- operational
- confident
- specific
